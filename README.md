## Which Dental Problems Need Immediate Attention?

Dental emergencies range from sudden injuries to infections that develop over several days. Knowing the common warning signs can help you decide when to contact a dentist.

**Persistent tooth pain:** A toothache that continues, worsens, or disrupts sleep may indicate deep decay, inflammation, or an infection inside the tooth.

**Broken or cracked teeth:** A fracture can expose sensitive layers of the tooth. Even if the damage appears small, an examination can establish whether the tooth needs repair.

**Swollen gums or facial swelling:** Swelling, pus, or a persistent unpleasant taste may indicate a dental abscess that requires prompt treatment.

**Knocked-out permanent teeth:** A permanent tooth that has been completely displaced needs immediate attention. Quick action can improve the possibility of saving it.

**Lost fillings or crowns:** A missing restoration can leave the underlying tooth exposed, causing sensitivity and increasing the risk of further damage.

**Bleeding after an injury:** Cuts to the lips, tongue, or gums may need professional attention, particularly when bleeding does not stop with gentle, continuous pressure.

Not every emergency requires a complex procedure. A dentist will assess the symptoms and determine whether the tooth can be preserved.

## When Should You Seek Urgent Dental Care?

Some symptoms should not be left until a routine check-up. Severe pain, increasing swelling, pain when biting, fever accompanied by dental symptoms and visible pus are reasons to seek prompt professional advice.

A toothache that suddenly stops does not always mean the problem has resolved. In some cases, the nerve inside a severely damaged tooth may have lost its vitality, while the underlying infection remains.

The report also recorded an average rate of 59.4 visits per 10,000 people annually. There are situations in which a hospital is more appropriate than a dental office. Difficulty breathing or swallowing, rapidly spreading facial or neck swelling, or signs of airway compromise require immediate emergency medical care.

## What Happens During an Emergency Dental Appointment?

The first step is usually an examination of the affected tooth, gums and surrounding tissues. Your dentist will ask about the symptoms, when they started and whether anything has made them better or worse. Dental X-rays may be needed to identify damage that is not visible during the examination.

Treatment depends on the diagnosis. A damaged tooth may be restored with a filling or crown. If the inner pulp is infected but the tooth can be saved, root canal treatment may be considered. An abscess may require drainage and treatment of the source of infection.

If a tooth is too damaged to repair, removal may be necessary. Your dentist should explain the reason for the recommended treatment and discuss suitable alternatives where available.

Before leaving, ask about pain management, aftercare, expected healing time and symptoms that would require a follow-up appointment. Clear instructions can help you recover safely.

## How Can You Protect a Tooth Before Seeing a Dentist?

The steps you take immediately after a dental injury can make a difference.

For a knocked-out permanent tooth, pick it up by the crown rather than the root. Do not scrub it or allow it to dry out. Keep it moist in milk or a suitable tooth-preservation solution and seek immediate dental care. Do not force it back into the socket.

For a chipped or broken tooth, rinse your mouth gently with water and avoid chewing on the damaged side. A cold compress against the cheek may help reduce swelling. If a piece of the tooth has broken off, keep it and take it to the appointment if possible.

For toothache, gentle salt-water rinsing and appropriate over-the-counter pain relief may provide temporary comfort. Follow medication instructions and check with a pharmacist or healthcare professional if you are unsure whether a medicine is safe for you.

Avoid placing aspirin directly on the gums, using sharp objects to examine the tooth or attempting to drain swelling yourself. These actions can cause additional harm.
